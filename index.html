Why the biggest number isn't the best number

Water holds about 1,600 PPB of dissolved hydrogen at atmospheric pressure. That is Henry's Law, not a manufacturing limit. Going higher means pressurising a sealed chamber, which is what these bottles do — and water above saturation is unstable. The excess starts escaping the moment generation stops and leaves faster once the lid is off.

So a 6,000 PPB claim describes a reading taken inside a sealed vessel. Three sips later that water is heading back toward 1,600 PPB. Published human studies mostly used 1,000 to 1,600 PPB, and above that more is not better in any way a drinker experiences.

Which is why this ranking is not a leaderboard of PPB figures. What separates these bottles is whether the number was measured, how long the bottle ran to reach it, how much water you get for the money, and who answers when something breaks.

A note on reviews

Review counts are the easiest number in this category to inflate. What is harder to fake is detail — and Hydrotech's 211 verified reviews, averaging 4.9, are unusually detailed.

Customers post their own photographs of the bottle in their kitchens and cars. They name the person who helped them. They write about the taste of the water, the weight of the glass, how the sleeve fits. Several are a year or more into owning one. A meaningful number came back to buy a second, or a third for family.

That is what a real review page reads like: specific, photographed, and written by people who clearly still have the product.
